# cv_satellite_img_classification

This repository demonstrates classical computer-vision feature engineering and machine learning for satellite image classification (no deep learning). The goal is to distinguish urban scenes from natural scenes using handcrafted features and classical classifiers.

## Problem Statement

Classify satellite images into two broad categories:
- **Urban** (e.g., Residential, Industrial, Highway)
- **Natural** / Rural (e.g., Forest, River, Pasture, SeaLake, Crops)

This project uses the EuroSAT dataset and intentionally avoids convolutional neural networks to illustrate traditional CV pipelines.

## Solution Overview

Pipeline steps:
- **Preprocessing:** Load, convert to RGB, resize, and convert to grayscale.
- **Feature Extraction:** Compute multiple complementary handcrafted feature types (color, micro-texture, macro-geometry, structural clutter, and frequency space).
- **Feature Normalization:** Standard scaling before training.
- **Model Training:** Train and evaluate classical ML classifiers.

## Handcrafted Features Used

To accurately separate complex built-up spaces from natural environments, the pipeline extracts a unified, 1D feature vector combining ten distinct traditional computer vision strategies:

### 1. Color Profiling
*   **Global Pixel Statistics (`extract_pixel_features`):** Computes global RGB means, standard deviations, and 32-bin normalized intensity histograms.
    *   *Satellite Context:* Instantly differentiates heavily built-up commercial centers (dominated by gray/white concrete and asphalt pixels) from deep forests or crop fields (dominated by high green channel frequencies).
*   **Vegetation & Color Indices (`extract_color_ratio_features`):** Calculates specialized remote sensing indices including an NDVI variant, Excess Green (ExG), Excess Red (ExR), and Excess Green-Minus-Red (ExGR).
    *   *Satellite Context:* Isolates crop plots and pastures from concrete residential zones. While clouds or fluctuating solar angles alter absolute brightness across frames, relative color ratios for active vegetation remain strongly decoupled from inert urban surfaces.


### 2. Texture Analysis
*   **Gray-Level Co-occurrence Matrices (`extract_multiscale_glcm_features`):** Extracts structural metrics (Contrast, Homogeneity, Energy, Correlation) across multiple pixel distances.
    *   *Satellite Context:* Distinguishes an unpaved rural field from a flat urban parking lot or industrial warehouse roof, even when both present identical global gray hues. The sandpaper-like micro-roughness of soil creates low homogeneity and high local contrast compared to smooth industrial materials.
*   **Local Binary Patterns (`extract_multiscale_lbp_features`):** Computes illumination-invariant local micro-texture histograms across multiple radii.
    *   *Satellite Context:* Identifies low-density residential suburbs vs. raw agricultural land. The uniform, alternating micro-textures of roof-to-lawn transitions produce distinct LBP signatures that remain highly stable across varying atmospheric haze.

### 3. Gradient & Macro-Geometry
*   **Histogram of Oriented Gradients (`extract_hog_features`):** Captures edge direction distributions inside localized cells across the canvas.
    *   *Satellite Context:* Detects gridded urban street networks, runway structures, and sharp structural building footprints. Because HOG models geometric silhouettes rather than color, it maps out the rigid, right-angled outlines characteristic of human engineering.
*   **Sobel Gradient Descriptors (`extract_sobel_features`):** Computes the mean and standard deviation of absolute first-order directional gradients.
    *   *Satellite Context:* Tracks sharp structural drops and artificial shadows. The steep, crisp gradient shifts generated by tall concrete building edges contrast deeply with the soft, gradual shadow gradients seen across natural rolling hills or flat pastures.

### 4. Structure & Spatial Context
*   **Geometric Line Features (`extract_line_features`):** Utilizes a Probabilistic Hough Line Transform to calculate segment counts, average lengths, and direction/orientation histograms.
    *   *Satellite Context:* Differentiates multi-lane highways or linear rail corridors from wandering country creeks and organic woodland boundaries. Human infrastructure registers as long, abundant linear segments locked into strict parallel or perpendicular orientation bins.
*   **Adaptive Edge Density (`extract_edge_density`):** Measures the absolute percentage of boundary pixels using an adaptive, median-driven Canny threshold combined with Hough tracking.
    *   *Satellite Context:* Quantifies visual clutter to isolate downtown city blocks from open prairie lands. Complex urban environments produce a dense web of sharp structural interfaces (shadow lines, structures, pavement seams), resulting in a high edge pixel ratio compared to minimal rural baselines.
*   **Spatial Grid Statistics (`extract_spatial_features`):** Subdivides the image into a coarse matrix layout (e.g., 4x4) to extract localized patch means and standard deviations.
    *   *Satellite Context:* Enforces a rough geographic composition rule onto traditional models without requiring expensive CNN architectures. For example, it helps the classifier learn typical urban fringe images where high-variance patches (suburban developments) sit directly alongside low-variance patches (adjacent wildlands).

### 5. Frequency Domain
*   **FFT Frequency Bands (`extract_frequency_features`):** Transforms spatial data into frequency space using a 2D Fast Fourier Transform, grouping energy levels into concentric circular frequency bands.
    *   *Satellite Context:* Flags dense metropolitan zones. The recurring, orderly structural spacing of city high-rises and tightly packed street grids creates powerful high-frequency spectral spikes, whereas rural terrains feature continuous, low-frequency natural transitions.

## Classification Models

The notebook trains and compares several classical classifiers:

- **Logistic Regression**
    - Simple linear baseline; fast and interpretable.
- **Random Forest**
    - Ensemble tree-based model; robust to feature scaling and useful for evaluating feature importance maps.
- **Support Vector Machine (SVM)**
    - Highly effective with handcrafted descriptors; RBF kernel used for parsing non-linear decision boundaries.

Evaluation metrics reported: accuracy, precision, recall, F1-score, and confusion matrix.

## How to Run
